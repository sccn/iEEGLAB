1. Install

  1. Install EEGLAB. Clone iEEGLAB into eeglab/plugins/, or download it and unzip it there: release 1.2.0 does not have all the steps below yet. From File > Manage EEGLAB extensions, install MEF3 (Part A) and bva-io (Part B). Part B draws the ECoG contacts on fsaverage: FreeSurfer has it, or get it once from Python with import mne; mne.datasets.fetch_fsaverage().
    Mac with Apple Silicon: compile the MEF3 reader (instructions).
  2. Start EEGLAB, then iEEGLAB > Check installation.
    ExpectAn iEEGLAB menu right of Help. Nothing required is missing. Help > iEEGLAB documentation (also at the bottom of the iEEGLAB menu) opens this page, the README, the tutorial script, and the help of each menu.
    Command line
    eeglab
ieeglab_check_install

2. Data

Nothing to download: two small extracts of OpenNeuro recordings ship with iEEGLAB, at their native sampling rate, each in one folder with its BIDS sidecar files: tutorial/seeg (ds004696 sub-02: 17 contacts, 3 stimulation sites, 34 pulses) and tutorial/ecog (ds004080 sub-ccepAgeUMCU02: 20 contacts, 3 sites, 30 pulses).

One participant per part, with the AWS command line (no account needed), or from ds004696 and ds004080. Keep the folder structure.

aws s3 sync --no-sign-request s3://openneuro.org/ds004696 ds004696 --exclude "*" --include "sub-02/*" --include "derivatives/freesurfer/sub-02/*" --include "dataset_description.json" --include "participants.tsv"
aws s3 sync --no-sign-request s3://openneuro.org/ds004080 ds004080 --exclude "*" --include "sub-ccepAgeUMCU02/ses-1/ieeg/*run-041456*" --include "sub-ccepAgeUMCU02/ses-1/ieeg/*_ses-1_electrodes.*" --include "sub-ccepAgeUMCU02/ses-1/ieeg/*_ses-1_coordsystem.json" --include "dataset_description.json" --include "participants.tsv"
The full sEEG recording needs about 20 GB of memory and CARLA takes 10 to 20 minutes. For a quicker run, read only a few stimulation sites at step 1 of Part A; the numbers then differ a little, also for the sites you keep, as CARLA and the permutations draw from one random stream.

5. Against FieldTrip

The same steps in FieldTrip (common average of the grids and bipolar depth leads with ft_preprocessing, then ft_freqanalysis with Hann windows of 0.2 s at 5 to 200 Hz every 10 ms, as the tutorial) give the same 145 channels and the same power: time-frequency r = 0.99999 (largest difference 0.03 in relative change), 70-150 Hz activity r = 1.0000, and the same ten strongest channels. The tutorial has no statistics; the test above is iEEGLAB's. Script: validation/validate_fieldtrip_uci29.m.

Stolk, A., Griffin, S., van der Meij, R., Dewar, C., Saez, I., Lin, J. J., Piantoni, G., Schoffelen, J.-M., Knight, R. T., & Oostenveld, R. (2018). Integrated analysis of anatomical and electrophysiological human intracranial data. Nature Protocols, 13(7), 1699-1723.

The analysis of Ojeda Valencia et al. (2023) on ds004696 sub-02, CRP of every stimulation site and recording contact and the connectivity matrix, run once with each re-referencing scheme of iEEGLAB and compared with the published result. The paper's own reference is a common average of the 20% of contacts with the lowest variance from 15 to 500 ms in each block of 64 recording channels (the published reference below). Full recording only. About 4.0 hours of computing in all, most of it ICA.

Expect gives the pairs that respond and how many of the 484 pairs of the published CRP get the same decision (315 respond there with the paper's test: the t-test at the response duration, Benjamini-Yekutieli correction over its pairs). The effect size d says how strong a response is: each trial projected onto the average of the other trials from 15 ms to 1 s, mean over standard deviation, so it rises when the response grows or its trial-to-trial variability falls. Its mean over the paper's responding pairs is given with a 95% confidence interval over the 20 stimulation sites.

4. Compared

At each main step: what re-referencing changes in the signal, which pairs respond, and the connectivity matrix, against the published result.

The scheme changes how many pairs respond, from 3495 with the recording's reference to 7165 with ICA with iEEGLAB's scaling. Against the paper, the decisions agree on 70% (ICA with iEEGLAB's scaling) to 86% (the common average) of its pairs; 83% with the paper's own reference. iEEGLAB finds 2 to 110 responses the paper does not, and misses 37 to 97 of its 315: the preprocessing and the test differ (permutation and false discovery rate over all 11172 pairs, against the paper's t-test corrected over its own pairs). Where both find a response, the response duration is closest with the published reference (median difference 2 ms). With the paper's own settings (no filtering, blanking or trial rejection, its reference applied before the baseline, its t-test and correction), iEEGLAB reaches the paper's decision on all 484 pairs. Changed one at a time, the test changes the most: iEEGLAB's permutation test does not find 58 to 67 of the paper's responses, as it repeats the choice of response duration that the t-test makes on the same data. Trial rejection changes 17 decisions, and re-referencing after the baseline, as in the steps above, 26: the paper's reference ranks contacts by their variance over all trials, which includes each trial's offset.

Effect sizes say how strong the responses are, not only whether they pass the test. On the 315 pairs that respond in the paper, the mean d is 2.95 with the published reference and largest with the common average (3.15). On the 169 that do not, it is highest with ICA with iEEGLAB's scaling (1.13, against -0.05). Against the published reference, with 95% confidence intervals: the recording's reference -0.59 [-0.93, -0.24] on the responding pairs and +0.04 [-0.10, +0.18] on the others; the common average +0.20 [+0.08, +0.32] on the responding pairs and +0.16 [+0.07, +0.25] on the others; CARLA +0.12 [+0.01, +0.23] on the responding pairs and +0.04 [-0.04, +0.12] on the others; ICA with iEEGLAB's scaling -0.14 [-0.42, +0.14] on the responding pairs and +1.17 [+0.86, +1.49] on the others; ICA with FieldTrip's scaling -0.28 [-0.52, -0.04] on the responding pairs and +0.29 [+0.20, +0.39] on the others. A scheme that raises d where the paper finds no response either spreads a signal across contacts or reveals responses the paper's reference hides; one that raises it only where the paper finds a response makes the responses stand out (with CARLA and the common average, mostly by lowering the trial-to-trial variability). The pairs without a response were chosen with the paper's reference, which favours it there. Selecting the pairs on half of the trials and measuring d on the other half, which favours no scheme, CARLA differs from the published reference by +0.08 [-0.01, +0.16] on the responding pairs and +0.08 [+0.04, +0.11] on the others.
